REPORT ON LAPTOP SALES, REPAIRS AND ACCESSORIES FOR THE MONTH OF AUGUST

Introduction

This report presents the activities recorded for the month of August regarding laptop sales, repairs, and accessories. A total of 28 laptops were sold, 32 laptops were brought in for repairs, and 29 accessories were recorded during the month.

Observations
	1.	Difficulty tracking repaired laptops:
I was unable to accurately track some laptops that had been repaired and were already collected by customers because there was no specific date recorded for when the laptop was received, repaired, or collected.
	2.	Lack of proper repair records:
The absence of dates and proper status updates made it difficult to determine which laptops had been fixed, which were still under repair, and which had already been collected.
	3.	Mix-up between laptops and accessories:
There was some confusion in the records because accessories were mixed up with laptop sales. This made it difficult to accurately separate the number of laptops sold from the accessories sold or supplied.
	4.	Inaccurate tracking of customer collections:
Without proper documentation of collection dates and customer details, it was difficult to confirm whether a repaired laptop was still in the shop or had already been collected.

Recommendations
	1.	Record the date for every repair:
Every laptop brought in for repair should have the date received, customer name, contact information, repair status, and date collected clearly recorded.
	2.	Use a repair tracking system:
A proper repair register or digital spreadsheet should be maintained with categories such as Received, Under Repair, Repaired, and Collected.
	3.	Separate laptop sales from accessories:
Laptop sales and accessories should have separate records to prevent confusion and make monthly calculations easier.
	4.	Use unique reference numbers:
Each repair should be given a unique job or receipt number. This will make it easier to locate the customer’s laptop and track its progress.
	5.	Update records immediately:
Whenever a laptop is repaired or collected, the record should be updated immediately to avoid missing information.
	6.	Carry out regular reconciliation:
At the end of every week or month, the physical laptops in the repair section should be compared with the records to identify missing, collected, or outstanding items.

Conclusion

The month of August recorded 28 laptops sold, 32 laptops received for repairs, and 29 accessories. However, the lack of proper date recording and the mixing of accessory records with laptop sales created challenges in accurately tracking the activities. Implementing a more organized recording and tracking system will improve accuracy, accountability, and efficiency in future month
